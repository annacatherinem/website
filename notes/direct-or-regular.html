Direct or regular: the same fund at two prices

23 November 2026

General education, not investment advice. I am not yet a SEBI-registered Investment Adviser.

Most mutual fund schemes in India come in two versions, a direct plan and a regular plan. In my experience, many investors have never been told which one they hold.

Both plans invest in the same portfolio and are run by the same fund manager. The difference is cost. A regular plan includes a commission that the fund house pays to the distributor who sold it, every year, for as long as you stay invested. A direct plan has no such commission, so its expense ratio is lower.

What the gap can cost

The difference is often between 0.5% and 1% a year. It looks small, and it compounds.

₹10,000 a month for 20 yearsValue at the end
Direct plan, assumed 12% a year₹99.9 lakh
Regular plan, assumed 11% a year₹87.4 lakh
Extra cost of the regular plan₹12.6 lakh

Illustration only, not a forecast or a promise of returns. Total invested is ₹24,00,000, paid at the start of each month and compounded monthly. The 12% is an assumption, and the regular plan is shown 1% lower to reflect its higher cost. Actual returns and costs will vary, and returns can be negative.

How to check yours

  1. Open your latest statement or your consolidated account statement.
  2. Read the full scheme name. It will say Direct or Regular.
  3. If it says Regular, compare the expense ratio of both plans on the fund house website.

If someone calls a switch an upgrade

If you hold a direct plan and someone suggests moving to the regular plan of the same fund, ask one question: what changes for me. The portfolio stays the same. The cost goes up, and a commission starts.

Ask what you pay, every year, and what you get for it.

A regular plan is a fair choice when you value the service of the person you bought it from, and you know what it costs. It should be your decision, made with the number in front of you.

One caution before you move anything. A switch between plans is treated as a sale and a fresh purchase, so exit load and capital gains tax can apply. Work that out first.
