About

Plans, not products.

Five years into financial planning, across three firms and two cities, one thing has stayed constant: most people arrive holding a few policies, a handful of funds, and no clear view of whether any of it adds up to the life they're working toward.

My job is to give them that view — what you own, what it costs, what it's for, and what happens next — and then a written plan that gets revisited as things change.

I handle end-to-end onboarding, risk profiling, goal mapping and portfolio construction, and I run periodic reviews and rebalancing against those goals. The approach is client-first and transparent, weighted toward long-term outcomes rather than product sales.

Alongside the CFP certification I hold an MBA in Financial Management from NMIMS, the QPFP designation from Network FP, and NISM Investment Adviser certifications at Levels 1 and 2.

Practice

The work, in six parts.

Comprehensive planning

One written plan covering income, spending, goals and the gap between them, with the trade-offs made explicit rather than buried.

Goal-based investing

Every rupee assigned a job and a date. Goals set the time horizon; the time horizon sets the risk.

Asset allocation & fund selection

Funds chosen against a client's goals and their tolerance for a bad year — not against last year's returns.

Portfolio review & rebalancing

Periodic reviews that check the portfolio still matches the plan, and correct the drift when it doesn't.

Retirement planning

A number to aim for, a path to it, and a withdrawal strategy for when the salary stops.

Insurance & risk management

What's actually covered, what's paid for twice, and what would happen to a family in the worst case.

Writing

Notes on planning.

I publish a carousel most weeks breaking down one piece of personal finance — the kind of thing that gets explained badly, or not at all, to the people it affects most.

It's the clearest picture of how I think about money, and the fastest way to tell whether we'd work well together.
