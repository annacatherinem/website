Financial planning questions, answered

Updated 26 November 2026

Plain answers to the questions I hear most often from salaried professionals, doctors, NRIs and business owners in Kerala.

This page is general education, not personal advice. I am not yet a SEBI-registered Investment Adviser. Tax rules change, so please confirm anything specific with a qualified professional.

What is a financial plan, and who needs one?

A financial plan is one written document that brings together your income, spending, savings, insurance, taxes, investments and retirement, and shows how each decision affects the others. Anyone with goals that cost money and take years to reach can benefit, whether that is a salaried professional, a doctor running a practice, an NRI family or a small-business owner.

What is the difference between a financial planner, an investment adviser and a distributor?

A financial planner looks at your whole financial picture. In India, anyone who gives investment advice for a fee must be registered with SEBI as an Investment Adviser (RIA). A mutual fund distributor or insurance agent sells products and is usually paid commission by the product provider. One person cannot act as both your adviser and your distributor for the same family.

How can I check whether someone is SEBI-registered?

SEBI publishes the list of registered Investment Advisers and Research Analysts on its website, under recognised intermediaries. Search by name or registration number. A registered adviser must show their registration number on their website and in their communication.

What does fee-only mean?

A fee-only planner is paid only by the client, through a fee agreed in advance, and earns no commission from any product. This removes the pull towards recommending products that pay more. SEBI sets limits on how much a registered Investment Adviser can charge.

How big should an emergency fund be?

There is no single right number. It depends on how stable your income is, how many people depend on you, your health cover, your loan payments and how quickly you could find work again. A doctor with a new practice and a salaried employee with two incomes at home will need very different amounts.

How much life insurance does a family need?

Enough to let your family meet its goals and clear its loans if your income stopped. Working it out means listing your loans, your family's yearly spending, the goals still ahead, and the savings and cover you already have. Formulas based on a multiple of salary often miss most of this.

Is the health insurance from my employer enough?

Employer cover usually ends when you leave the job or retire, which is exactly when buying a new policy can be harder or costlier. Many families keep a personal health policy alongside the employer cover for this reason. It is worth checking what your employer policy covers for parents, waiting periods and room rent limits.

What is the difference between an NRE and an NRO account?

An NRE account holds money earned abroad and brought into India. It is freely repatriable, and its interest is tax-free in India. An NRO account holds income earned in India, such as rent or dividends. Its interest is taxable in India, and repatriation is allowed within yearly limits with the required paperwork.

What changes when an NRI moves back to Kerala?

Your residential status changes, and with it how your income is taxed in India. Some returning NRIs qualify for a transition status called RNOR, during which most foreign income is not taxed in India. Your bank accounts also need to be redesignated, and foreign assets may need to be reported in your tax return. The details depend on your days of stay, so confirm them with a tax professional.

How are RSUs and ESOPs from a foreign employer taxed?

When RSUs vest or ESOPs are exercised, the value is usually taxed as part of your salary. When you later sell the shares, the gain is taxed as a capital gain. Shares held abroad generally need to be reported in the foreign assets section of your income tax return, even if you have not sold them.

Why do nominations and a will matter if I am young?

A nominee receives the money, but a will decides who it finally belongs to. Without both, a family can face months of paperwork at the hardest time. Checking nominations on every bank account, demat account, mutual fund, insurance policy and provident fund is one of the simplest steps in any plan.

How often should a financial plan be reviewed?

At least once a year, and whenever life changes: a new job, marriage, a child, a home loan, a move abroad or back, or a health event. A review checks whether the plan still matches your goals, not whether you should chase whatever did well last year.
